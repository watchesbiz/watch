# Storage Environment: What Actually Degrades a Stored Watch

Four variables matter, in this order: **humidity, temperature stability, light, and vibration.** Everything else people worry about (dust, air exposure, "letting it breathe") is secondary or imaginary.

This page gives workable ranges and, more usefully, tells you how to measure your actual storage spot — because the range that matters is the one inside your drawer, not the one on a climate chart.

---

## 1. Humidity — the variable that does real damage

`[COMMON]` The usual recommendation is **40–60% relative humidity (RH)**. The reasoning behind the two ends is different, which is why both matter:

| RH | What happens | Time scale |
|---|---|---|
| Below 35% | Leather straps dry out and crack at the lug ends; lubricant films lose their volatile fraction faster | Months |
| 40–60% | Stable. No mould, no drying, gasket rubber stays elastic | — |
| Above 65% | Mould on leather and fabric; moisture migrates past aged gaskets; dial lacquer and printed markings can bloom | Weeks to months |
| Above 80% | Steel cases develop surface corrosion in the gasket channels; plated cases pit | Weeks |

**Measure it, don't assume it.** A £10 hygrometer left inside the drawer or box for 24 hours tells you more than any general advice. Three places that routinely surprise people:

- **Inside a safe.** Safes are sealed boxes. Moisture from the room accumulates and has nowhere to go. A safe in an unheated room can sit at 70%+ while the room reads 50%.
- **Bathroom cabinets.** Shower steam pushes local RH past 80% daily. This is the worst common storage spot and the most popular one.
- **Basements and garages.** Stable temperature, but RH tracks the season.

### If RH is too high

Silica gel works, but only if you regenerate it. Sachets saturate in weeks and then **hold** moisture rather than absorb it. Either use indicating gel (colour change) and dry it on a radiator monthly, or use a small rechargeable dehumidifier unit for safes and cabinets.

Do not use chemical dehumidifiers that release scent, and **never store watches with mothballs or camphor-based insect repellent.** Camphor vapour attacks plastic components, gasket rubber, and the lubricant film — it is one of the few storage mistakes that causes permanent internal damage.

### If RH is too low

Below 30% is rare outside heated winters, but it does happen in centrally heated flats. The risk is leather, not the movement. Store leather-strapped watches in a closed box rather than open air if you run a dehumidifier hard.

## 2. Temperature — stability beats the number

`[COMMON]` **18–25 °C, as steady as possible.** Mechanical watches survive wider ranges than people expect; what they dislike is change.

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Slow drift within 15–28 °C | Rate shifts slightly (a few seconds/day). Harmless |
| Rapid change, e.g. cold car → warm room | Condensation forms on the case and, if the crown is out or a gasket is aged, inside the case |
| Sustained above 35 °C | Lubricant viscosity drops, rate drifts; long-term, oil oxidises faster |
| Below 5 °C | Rate slows noticeably; not damaging in itself, but avoid winding a cold movement hard |
| Freezing | Do not. Crystal gaskets can deform |

The practical rule: **do not store watches anywhere you would not store a laptop battery.** That single test excludes attics, cars, garages in summer, and the shelf above a radiator.

A watch on a winder is a small heat source in a closed box — the motor and the movement both produce a little warmth, and in an unvented safe or a closed drawer that adds up. See [TRAVEL-AND-CASES](TRAVEL-AND-CASES.md) for the safe-and-winder problem specifically.

## 3. Light — slow, permanent, and mostly about the dial

`[COMMON]` UV does two things, both irreversible:

- **Fades and shifts dial colour** — blue and green dials go greyish, red accents go brown. Sunlight through a window is enough over years.
- **Ages lume** — tritium-painted dials (pre-1998 mostly) discolor to cream then brown; modern Super-LumiNova is far more stable but not immune. Ironically, collectors now pay a premium for aged tritium, so "damage" depends on the watch.
- **Bleaches leather** and dries it at the fold.

`[JUDGEMENT]` Keep watches out of direct sun. A drawer, a closed box, or a display case away from a window is fine. Display cases on a sunny windowsill are the single most common cause of a faded dial on an otherwise excellent watch.

---

## 4. Vibration — rarely the problem people think

`[COMMON]` A watch at rest is not damaged by normal household vibration: doors, footsteps, washing machines in the next room. The movement is designed to survive being worn while you run.

Two exceptions:

- **A winder on an unstable surface.** If the whole unit rocks, the winding cycles are no longer clean and the rotor gets shock-loaded. Put it on a solid surface, level. More on placement in [LONG-TERM-STORAGE](LONG-TERM-STORAGE.md).
- **Storing loose watches together in one compartment.** Cases knocking against each other for months will mark polished flanks and scratch crystals. Individual slots or pouches; this is most of the reason multi-slot winders exist.

---

## Quick audit: score your storage spot

Answer these five. Two or more "no" answers means move the watch.

| Check | Pass looks like |
|---|---|
| RH between 40% and 60%, measured over 24 h | Hygrometer reading, not a guess |
| No daily steam exposure | Not a bathroom, not next to a kettle or shower |
| Temperature steady within roughly 15–28 °C | Not a windowsill, radiator shelf, attic, or car |
| No direct sun on the dial | Shade or closed container |
| No camphor / mothball / scented dehumidifier in the same space | Nothing that off-gasses into the case |

---

## What does *not* matter

- **"Watches need air."** They do not. A closed box is better than open air.
- **Dust.** Cosmetically annoying on the crystal, mechanically irrelevant with the crown screwed in.
- **Storing a watch next to other watches.** No magnetic risk in practice unless one of them has a magnet in the clasp or caseback (some hybrid smartwatches do).
- **Altitude.** Normal travel altitude is a non-issue; pressure-change damage comes from diving, not flights.
